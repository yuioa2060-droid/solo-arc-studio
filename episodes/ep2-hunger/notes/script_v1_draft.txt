You did what I said. You ate a little less. And by evening… all you can think about is food. That's not weakness. It's not a lack of discipline. It's your body doing exactly what it was built to do. When you eat less, it turns up the hunger alarm. The good news? That alarm has dials. Four of them. Today I'll show you how to turn each one down. And at the end, you'll get one full day of eating you can copy tomorrow. Let's get started.

First, why is it so loud? In one famous study, people lost weight on a very strict diet. A year later, their hunger signals were still turned up… and their fullness signals still turned down. Their bodies were still pushing them to eat. Now, that was an extreme diet. A small gap pushes back much less. But it never pushes back zero. So you can't switch hunger off. You can only turn it down.

Dial number one. Protein. In one study, people ate fifteen percent of their calories as protein. Then thirty. Nobody told them to eat less. And without counting a single calorie, they ate about four hundred and forty fewer calories a day. So plan protein first. Eggs. Greek yogurt. Chicken. Fish. Beans. A palm-sized portion at every meal. Especially breakfast, where most people have almost none.

Dial number two. Volume. Your stomach can't count calories. It only feels how much is in there. In one lab study, people ate a whole apple, applesauce, or apple juice. Same fruit. About the same calories. Then they had lunch. The whole apple made them eat the least. The juice did almost nothing. So eat your calories. Don't drink them. And start your biggest meals with a big bowl of vegetables or soup. In a year-long study, women who ate more of these foods lost more weight… and felt less hungry doing it.

Dial number three. How your food is made. In one hospital study, twenty adults lived on two diets for two weeks. Same calories on the menu. Same sugar, fat and fiber. One was mostly real food. The other was mostly ultra-processed. On the processed diet, people ate about five hundred more calories a day… and gained weight. On real food, they lost it. They also ate faster. So build most meals from food that still looks like what it came from. Potatoes. Rice. Eggs. Meat. Fruit. Oats.

Dial number four. Your evening. In one study, young men slept four hours a night for just two nights. Their hunger signal went up. Their fullness signal went down. They felt about a quarter hungrier, and wanted sweet, starchy food most. And here's what nobody warns you about. Even with good sleep, your body clock makes you hungriest in the evening. So don't fight it with willpower. Plan for it. Make dinner your biggest protein meal. Keep one planned snack with some volume, like yogurt and berries. Then close the kitchen.

One last trick. Drink half a liter of water before your two biggest meals. In one twelve-week trial, people who did that lost about one and a half kilos more.

So here's your day. Breakfast: protein first. Lunch: a big bowl of vegetables before anything else. Dinner: real food, with protein, and a glass of water before. Evening: one planned snack… then a short walk. And bed, at the same time every night. Screenshot it.

One honest warning. A little hunger is normal. Mild hunger before a meal is a signal, not an emergency. But if you feel dizzy, weak, or can't stop thinking about food, your gap is too big. Eat a little more. And if you have a medical condition, talk to your doctor before you change how you eat. So tonight, do one thing. Decide tomorrow's breakfast. Protein first.
